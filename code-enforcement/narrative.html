What the data shows

Long Beach's portal logs every code-enforcement request — a neighbor's complaint, a routine inspection, a proactive sweep. Most were investigated and closed with nothing found. Of 142,233 requests since 2008, only 48,418 (34%) became confirmed code cases with a violation.

Read it right: the map and rankings default to the 48,418 confirmed violations. Toggle + Complaints to see all enforcement activity — useful, but two-thirds of it found no violation.

Who's behind them

Nearly a third of confirmed violations — 29.5% — sit on property held by LLCs, corporations, and limited partnerships, with more under family trusts. The Owners on the hook panel ranks the named entities by violations found, and re-ranks to any single district. Individual homeowners stay anonymous; registered business owners are public record and named.

What kind, and when

Among cases the city categorized, property-maintenance issues — overgrown lots, trash, inoperative vehicles, deteriorated exteriors — dominate, alongside unpermitted construction and habitability complaints. Volume was heaviest 2016–2020. One honest gap: for 44% of cases the city recorded no violation type at all, so those read "No type recorded" — the city's omission, not a missing scrape.

Privacy by design: the map shows density, not addresses — a ~110-meter grid. You can see where problems cluster, but no individual resident or unit is ever pinpointed. Accountability lands on owners, not tenants.
